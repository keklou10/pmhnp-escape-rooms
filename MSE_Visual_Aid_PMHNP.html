1
Appearance
Static, observable features — "if you can capture it in a photo, it's appearance"
💡 Key rule: Photo = Appearance · Movie = Behavior. A frozen facial expression (blank stare) is appearance; shifting expressions throughout the interview is behavior.

🧍 General

  • Gender, stated age
  • Appears stated / older / younger
  • Body type: ecto, meso, endomorph
  • Underweight / overweight / muscular

👕 Clothing

  • Formal vs. casual vs. hospital gown
  • Clean / dirty / torn / stained
  • Mismatched, weather-inappropriate
  • Colors, patterns, style

🧼 Hygiene

  • Body odor, urine/feces odor
  • Food stains (bib area)
  • Well-groomed vs. unkempt
  • Perfume / cologne present

💇 Hair & Face

  • Short / long / bald / matted
  • Lanugo hair (anorexia clue)
  • Facial hair: beard / unshaven
  • Makeup: careful / carelessly done / excessive

👁️ Eye Contact

  • Sustained, downcast gaze
  • Avoiding eye contact entirely
  • Staring through interviewer
  • (If changes during interview → Behavior)

🪪 ID Markings

  • Scars, bruises, tattoos
  • Needle tracks (IV drug use)
  • Piercings, jewelry
  • Missing teeth

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Appearance

MDDPoor grooming, poor hygiene, psychomotor changes visible
AnorexiaLow body weight, baggy clothes, lanugo hair on skin
ManiaExcessive makeup, jewelry, bright/bizarre clothing
SUD – ETOHArcus senilis, bruising, palmar erythema, spider angiomata
SUD – IVNeedle tracks, long sleeves to hide marks
DementiaPoor grooming, food stains on bib, body odor
TrichotillomaniaPatchy hair loss, absent eyebrows
2
Behavior
Dynamic observations — psychomotor level + attitude toward interviewer
⚡ Psychomotor Level

Agitated / Increased

Tapping foot · shifting in chair · rocking · squirming · hand gesturing · hand wringing · pacing · drumming fingers · unable to sit still

Calm / Decreased

Sitting quietly · slowed movements · minimal spontaneous movement · catatonic posturing · stooped / hunched posture

Involuntary Movements

  • Grimacing, tics, twitches, blinking
  • Lip smacking (Tardive Dyskinesia)
  • Hand tremor (Lithium/Depakote)
  • Akathisia — restlessness from DA blockade
  • Dystonia — abrupt muscle spasms
  • Echopraxia — imitating another's movements
  • Asterixis — "flapping" tremor
🤝 Attitude Toward Interviewer

Cooperative

Appropriate, compliant, engaged, open

Guarded

Evasive, withdrawn, aloof, passive, disinterested

Hostile

Defensive, aggressive, manipulative, threatening, demanding

Unusual

Seductive, provocative, condescending, suspicious, puzzled

✏️ Contextualize! "Scowling while describing how family insisted he get help" is far more descriptive than simply "scowling."

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Behavior

MDDPsychomotor retardation or agitation, withdrawn
ManiaDemanding, provocative, seductive, restless, distractible
OCDRepetitive ritualistic behaviors visible
GADMuscle tension, fidgety, sweaty palms
ASPDCalm, glib, manipulative, charming
PsychosisScanning room (paranoid?), responding to internal stimuli
3
Mood
The patient's subjective, sustained emotional "climate" — use their own words
🌡️ Mood vs. Affect: Mood is the persistent emotional climate (weeks–months). Affect is the fluctuating emotional weather (minute-to-minute). Ask directly: "How has your mood been lately?"
Types of Mood

😢 Dysphoric

Unpleasant mood — sadness, anxiety, or irritability. Umbrella term for distressing mood states.

🌟 Elevated

Exaggerated well-being, euphoria, elation. Patient may say "on top of the world" or "high."

😊 Euthymic

Normal mood range — absence of depressed or elevated mood. This is the baseline.

😤 Irritable

Easily annoyed and provoked to anger. Common in mania, MDD, PTSD, and borderline PD.

🌈 Expansive

Lack of restraint in expressing feelings; over-valuation of one's significance. Seen in mania.

🤷 Alexithymia

Patient cannot describe or identify their own mood/emotional state.

📝 Documentation tip: Always quote the patient's own words in quotation marks when recording mood. E.g., Mood: "pretty down, like I'm dragging through mud" — more informative than "depressed."

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Mood

ManiaElevated, expansive, or irritable mood (DSM-5-TR criterion A)
MDDDysphoric, hopeless, low energy; may be irritable
BPDChronic emptiness + rapid dysphoric mood shifts
GADAnxious, apprehensive baseline mood
4
Affect
Observable expression of emotion — 4 components: State · Range · Appropriateness · Congruence
The 4 Components of Affect

① State

Your assessment of the patient's current emotional presentation:
Dysphoric · Euthymic · Euphoric · Irritable · Anxious · Guarded

③ Appropriateness

Does the affect match the content being discussed?
Appropriate: tearful discussing loss
Inappropriate: laughing when discussing death

④ Congruence

Does the affect match the stated mood?
Incongruent example: patient states mood is "fine" but is continuously tearful throughout the interview

② Range Spectrum

Full
Restricted
Blunted
Flat
FullNormal range of emotional expression (culturally contextualized)
RestrictedMild reduction in range/intensity of expression
BluntedSignificant reduction in emotional expression intensity
FlatAbsent or near-absent affective expression (classic schizophrenia)
LabileAbrupt, rapid, extreme variability — repeated mood shifts
GuardedSimilar to restricted; patient actively appears to hide emotions

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Affect

ManiaElevated/expansive mood + labile affect range
SchizophreniaBlunted or flat affect (negative symptom cluster)
MDDRestricted, constricted, or guarded affect
BPDLabile affect + chronic dysphoria + intense anger
Delirium (Hyperactive)Labile affect range
Delirium (Hypoactive)Blunted affect range
5
Speech
Clarity · Rate · Quantity · Tone · Flow · Volume — describe all relevant dimensions

🔊 Clarity

  • Clear / coherent
  • Slurred (dysarthric)
  • Mumbling / stuttering
  • Garbled / stammering
  • Accent present

⏱️ Rate

  • Slow
  • Relaxed / conversational
  • Rapid
  • Pressured (cannot interrupt)

📊 Quantity

  • Talkative / verbose
  • Increased / pressured
  • Minimal / decreased
  • Mute

🎵 Tone & Prosody

  • Monotonous / flat
  • Animated / expressive
  • Emphatic / excited
  • Sullen / irritated
  • Prosody = rate + volume + tone

🌊 Flow

  • Spontaneous vs. hesitant
  • Word-finding difficulties
  • Delayed response / latency
  • Fluent vs. non-fluent

📣 Volume

  • Whispered
  • Soft / quiet
  • Conversational
  • Loud / yelling
⚡ Pressured Speech: Increased quantity, accelerated, nearly impossible to interrupt. The patient "verbally runs you over." Often loud, emphatic, may continue even with no listener. Classic mania finding.

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Speech

ManiaPressured speech — or rapid with increased quantity
AnxietyIncreased quantity, rapid rate
MDDDecreased quantity, soft, monotone, reduced prosody
SchizophreniaDecreased quantity, monotone, non-spontaneous, increased latency
Substance intoxSlurred (dysarthric), garbled speech

✅ Goal-Directed / Logical

Answers the question; clear word/sentence structure; logical linkage between ideas; gets to the point

🌀 Circumstantial

Over-inclusive of irrelevant details — eventually reaches the point but takes a long winding path

↗️ Tangential

Never reaches the original point; goes off on topics triggered by internal/external stimuli; words are understandable

🚀 Flight of Ideas

Rapid, near-continuous speech with abrupt topic changes based on understandable word associations; listener can usually follow the links; seen with pressured speech in mania

🌪️ Loose Associations

Connection between ideas is unclear or nonsensical; listener cannot follow topic changes; substantially impairs communication. Classic in schizophrenia

  • Fragmentation: phrases intact but disconnected — meaningless sentences
  • Word salad: words intact but all sentence structure lost
  • Incoherence: unintelligible words; no phrases or structure
Rate & Flow Abnormalities
  • Racing thoughts: too fast to articulate — "like 3 TVs at once"
  • Perseveration: repeating same verbal responses despite new questions
  • Thought blocking: abrupt interruption before idea completes; then resumes
  • Derailment: speech stops suddenly, restarts on unrelated topic
📋 Document proportionally: "Thought process mostly logical and sequential, with ~25% tangentiality" is more useful than a binary label.

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Thought Process

SchizophreniaDerailment, loose associations, fragmentation, word salad (if severe)
ManiaRacing thoughts + flight of ideas
DeliriumAny disorder of thought process
DementiaErrors in word choice and grammar
7
Thought Content
What the patient is thinking — 6 critical domains to assess

🚨 A. Suicidal Ideation

Thoughts of DeathContemplates implications; thinks about funeral; passive musing
Passive SI"Wishes to go to sleep and not wake up" — no intent to act
Active SIWants to die now; with or without specific plan (plan details → HPI)

⚠️ B. Homicidal Ideation

ThoughtsThinks about killing/hurting another; considers implications
Active HIWants to kill a specific person now; plan details documented in HPI

🧠 C. Delusions — False fixed beliefs held despite contradicting evidence

Persecutory/Paranoid
Being attacked, harassed, conspired against — most common
Referential
Random events have special personal significance ("the Cubs losing is a signal for me")
Grandiose
Inflated worth, power, special relationship to deity or celebrity
Jealousy
Sexual partner is being unfaithful
Erotomanic
A higher-status person is in love with the patient
Somatic
False beliefs involving bodily functions or sensations
🔴 Bizarre delusions = physically impossible by cultural standards: Thought withdrawal (others stealing thoughts) · Thought insertion (foreign thoughts implanted) · Thought broadcasting (others can hear thoughts) · Delusion of control (external force controls actions)

👁️ D. Hallucinations

AuditoryMust be in clear sensorium — command AH (can patient resist?)
VisualFalse visual perceptions — common in delirium, substance states
TactileFormication: bugs crawling under skin (cocaine intoxication)
OlfactoryFalse smell perceptions
GustatoryFalse taste perceptions
Hypna/pompicWhile falling asleep (hypnagogic) or waking (hypnopompic) — NOT abnormal
Mnemonic: Hypnagogic = going to sleep · Hypnopompic = morning wakeup

🔁 E. Obsessions & Compulsions

Obsessions: intrusive, ego-dystonic thoughts causing distress (contamination, doubt, harm, sexual imagery)

Compulsions: repetitive behaviors or mental acts aimed at reducing obsession-driven anxiety (washing, checking, counting)

🌫️ F. Derealization & Depersonalization

Derealization: surroundings feel unreal, dreamlike, foggy, visually distorted

Depersonalization: detachment from one's own body/mind — "watching myself from outside"

These typically co-occur and may represent different facets of the same dissociative phenomenon. Common in PTSD.

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Thought Content

SchizophreniaBizarre delusions, AH (often command), thought insertion/broadcasting
OCDEgo-dystonic obsessions + compulsions
PTSDFlashbacks, derealization, depersonalization
Cocaine intoxFormication (tactile hallucinations — "coke bugs")
ETOH withdrawalHallucinosis (knows they're not real = insight intact)
Delusional DisorderNon-bizarre delusions without other psychotic symptoms
8
Cognition
Orientation · Level of consciousness · Attention · Memory · Abstract thinking · Fund of knowledge
🧭 Oriented × 4
Person — Does the patient know who they are and who is around them? (Only absent in most severe cases)
Place — Does the patient know where they are AND behave accordingly?
Time — Approximate date and time of day; does behavior match?
Situation — Oriented to the purpose of the interview; overlaps with insight
💡 Level of Consciousness
Alert
Fully awake, responsive
Clouding
Impaired environmental awareness
Somnolent
Drowsy, delayed/incomplete responses
Stupor
Vigorous, repeated stimulation required
Coma
No verbal or motor response to noxious stimuli
🧩 Cognitive Domains

Concentration & Attention

Serial 7s (100–7–7…) · Spell WORLD / EARTH forwards then backwards

Memory

  • Recent: recall news events from past months
  • Remote: childhood data, personal history (verify!)
  • Recall: 3 unrelated words after 3–5 min (e.g., apple · honesty · star)

Abstract Thinking

Proverb interpretation ("a rolling stone gathers no moss") · Similarities & differences ("how are a bush and a tree alike?") · Concrete = takes everything literally

Formal Cognitive Screens

MMSE (Folstein) — 30 points · SLUMS · MoCA — all validated for bedside use

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Cognition

DeliriumDisoriented to time/place/situation (rarely person); poor concentration; serial MMSE testing
DementiaMemory impairment, poor abstraction, apraxia, agnosia
MDDPoor concentration, indecisive — can mimic dementia ("pseudodementia")
ManiaDistractible, poor concentration despite high energy
KorsakoffAnterograde amnesia — poor/variable short-term memory; confabulation
Substance intoxDecreased alertness, disoriented, poor concentration
9
Judgment
Current/recent ability to assess situations correctly and act appropriately within reality
⚖️ Judgment examines recent decisions and choices, not hypothetical problem-solving. Asking about real behavior ("What did you do when…?") is more clinically relevant than the theater-fire hypothetical.

What to Assess

  • Recent choices that led to current presentation
  • Ability to make plans consistent with reality
  • Response to hypothetical scenarios (less preferred)
  • Awareness of consequences of behavior

Rating Scale

Good / IntactDecisions appropriate; acts within social/safety norms
Fair / PartialSome appropriate decisions; inconsistent; limited follow-through
Poor / ImpairedDecisions clearly inconsistent with reality or safety norms
⚠️ Critical point: A patient who attempted suicide 3 hours ago and is now denying SI — judgment cannot be described as "good" in isolation. Always contextualize with recent behavior. Document: "Judgment currently appears intact; however, patient demonstrated poor judgment as evidenced by suicide attempt earlier today."

📌 Poor Judgment Examples

  • Acquiring weapon to self-harm
  • Acting on delusions (calling exterminator for formication)
  • Spending thousands during manic episode
  • Stopping medications without discussing

✍️ Documentation Examples

  • "Judgment: Poor — patient actively delusional, planning to harm identified individual"
  • "Judgment: Fair/Poor — currently denies delusions but was actively delusional and acting on beliefs as recently as yesterday"
  • "Judgment: Fair — patient acknowledges need for treatment but has not followed through with medication adherence"
10
Insight
To what degree does the patient understand that they are ill — and can act on that understanding?
Insight Spectrum
POOR
🚫
Complete DenialRefuses to acknowledge illness; blames others, external factors, or organic causes for symptoms
PARTIAL
🌗
Partial AwarenessAcknowledges something is wrong but simultaneously denies it; ambivalent about needing help
Intellectual InsightUnderstands cognitively that they are ill but cannot apply this awareness to make behavioral changes
GOOD / FULL
✅
True InsightUnderstands objective reality of situation + has motivation and emotional impetus to make useful behavioral changes; working with treatment team

✍️ Documentation Examples

  • "Insight: Poor — pt stopped medications, now actively manic, refusing treatment"
  • "Insight: Fair — pt acknowledges depression and SI but declines treatment ('just doesn't want it')"
  • "Insight: Fair — pt agrees to help after involuntary admission last night"
  • "Insight: Good — pt demonstrates clear understanding of illness and is actively engaged in treatment planning"

🔑 Key Principle

In psychiatry, the default is that Judgment and Insight are intact. You are assessing for evidence of impairment, not trying to prove them good.

A single area of impaired judgment (e.g., actively suicidal) can have dire consequences — document thoroughly even when overall functioning appears intact.

📌 Always consider both current and recent events when rating Judgment and Insight — the two are closely related and should be assessed together.
