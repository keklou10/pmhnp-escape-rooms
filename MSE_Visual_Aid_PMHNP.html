1
Appearance
Static, observable features — "If you can capture it in a photo, it's appearance"
💡

Key rule: Photo = Appearance · Movie = Behavior. A frozen blank stare is appearance; shifting expressions throughout the interview are behavior.

🧍 General
  • Gender, stated age
  • Appears stated / older / younger
  • Body type: ecto · meso · endomorph
  • Underweight / overweight / muscular
👕 Clothing
  • Formal vs. casual vs. hospital gown
  • Clean / dirty / torn / stained / frayed
  • Mismatched or weather-inappropriate
  • Colors, patterns, style choices
🧼 Hygiene
  • Body odor, urine/feces odor
  • Food stains (bib area of shirt)
  • Well-groomed vs. disheveled
  • Perfume or cologne present
💇 Hair & Face
  • Short / long / bald / matted
  • Lanugo hair — anorexia clue
  • Facial hair: beard / unshaven
  • Makeup: careful / carelessly done / excessive
👁️ Eye Contact
  • Sustained, downcast gaze
  • Avoiding eye contact entirely
  • Staring through interviewer
  • Changes during interview → Behavior
🪪 ID Markings
  • Scars, bruises, tattoos
  • Needle tracks (IV drug use)
  • Piercings, jewelry, missing teeth
  • Clothing hiding marks
🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Appearance
MDDPoor grooming, poor hygiene, appears older than stated age
Anorexia NervosaLow body weight, baggy clothes, lanugo hair
Mania / HPDExcessive makeup, jewelry, brightly colored or bizarre clothing
SUD – ETOHArcus senilis, bruising, palmar erythema, spider angiomata
SUD – IVNeedle tracks; long sleeves worn to conceal
DementiaPoor grooming, food stains on bib area, body odor
TrichotillomaniaPatchy hair loss; absent or sparse eyebrows
2
Behavior
Dynamic observations — psychomotor level + attitude toward interviewer
✏️

Contextualize! "Scowling while describing how family insisted he get help" is far more informative than simply "scowling."

⚡ Psychomotor Level

Agitated / Increased

Tapping foot · shifting in chair · rocking · squirming · hand gesturing · hand wringing · pacing · drumming fingers · nail biting

Calm / Decreased

Sitting quietly · slowed movements · minimal spontaneous movement · catatonic posturing · stooped posture

Involuntary Movements

  • Grimacing, tics, twitches, blinking
  • Lip smacking — Tardive Dyskinesia
  • Hand tremor (Lithium, Depakote, Parkinsonism)
  • Akathisia — restlessness from DA blockade
  • Dystonia — abrupt muscle spasms/stiffness
  • Echopraxia — imitating another's movements
  • Asterixis — "flapping" tremor
🤝 Attitude Toward Interviewer
✅ Cooperative

Appropriate, compliant, open, engaged

⚠️ Guarded

Evasive, aloof, withdrawn, disinterested

🚫 Hostile

Aggressive, manipulative, threatening, demanding

❓ Unusual

Seductive, provocative, suspicious, puzzled

👁️

Eye contact variants: Intermittent · irregular · staring at interviewer · staring through interviewer · shifty · avoidant · scanning room (paranoid?)

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Behavior
MDDPsychomotor retardation or agitation; withdrawn
ManiaDemanding, provocative, seductive, restless, distractible
OCDRepetitive, ritualistic behaviors visible during interview
GADMuscle tension, fidgety, sweaty palms
ASPDCalm, glib, charming, manipulative
PsychosisScanning room (paranoid?); responding to internal stimuli
3
Mood
Subjective, sustained emotional "climate" — in the patient's own words
🌡️

Mood vs. Affect: Mood is the persistent emotional climate (weeks–months). Affect is the fluctuating weather (minute-to-minute). Ask: "How has your mood been lately?"

Types of Mood
😢 Dysphoric

Unpleasant mood — sadness, anxiety, or irritability. Umbrella term for distressing mood states.

🌟 Elevated

Exaggerated well-being, euphoria. "On top of the world," "high," "ecstatic."

😊 Euthymic

Normal mood range — absence of depressed or elevated mood. Baseline.

😤 Irritable

Easily annoyed and provoked to anger. Common in mania, MDD, PTSD, BPD.

🌈 Expansive

Lack of restraint; over-valuation of one's significance. Seen in mania.

🤷 Alexithymia

Patient cannot describe or identify their own mood/emotional state.

📝 Documentation tip: Always quote the patient's own words in quotation marks. "Pretty down, like dragging through mud" is far more informative than "depressed."
🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Mood
ManiaElevated, expansive, or irritable (DSM-5-TR Criterion A)
MDDDysphoric, hopeless, low energy; may be irritable
BPDChronic emptiness + rapid dysphoric mood shifts
GADPersistently anxious, apprehensive baseline mood
4
Affect
Observable expression of emotion — 4 components to assess
The 4 Components
① State

Your assessment of the patient's current emotional presentation: Dysphoric · Euthymic · Euphoric · Irritable · Anxious · Guarded

③ Appropriateness

Appropriate: tearful discussing loss; laughing at something funny.
Inappropriate: laughing when discussing death; overly flirtatious with interviewer.

④ Congruence

Does affect match the stated mood? Incongruent example: patient says mood is "fine" but is continuously tearful throughout the interview.

② Range Spectrum

Blunted
Flat
FullNormal emotional expression (culturally contextualized)
RestrictedMild reduction in range/intensity of expression

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Affect
ManiaElevated/expansive mood + labile affect range
SchizophreniaBlunted or flat affect (negative symptom cluster)
MDDRestricted, constricted, or guarded affect
BPDLabile affect + chronic dysphoria + intense anger
Delirium (Hyperactive)Labile affect range
Delirium (Hypoactive)Blunted affect; appears sedated
5
Speech
Clarity · Rate · Quantity · Tone · Flow · Volume
🔊 Clarity
  • Clear / coherent
  • Slurred (dysarthric)
  • Mumbling / stuttering
  • Garbled / stammering
⏱️ Rate
  • Slow
  • Relaxed / conversational
  • Rapid
  • Pressured — cannot interrupt
📊 Quantity
  • Talkative / verbose
  • Increased / pressured
  • Minimal / decreased
  • Mute
🎵 Tone & Prosody
  • Monotonous / flat
  • Animated / expressive
  • Emphatic / excited
  • Prosody = rate + volume + tone
🌊 Flow
  • Spontaneous vs. hesitant
  • Word-finding difficulties
  • Delayed response / latency
  • Fluent vs. non-fluent
📣 Volume
  • Whispered
  • Soft / quiet
  • Conversational
  • Loud / yelling
⚡ Pressured Speech: Increased quantity, accelerated, nearly impossible to interrupt. The patient "verbally runs you over." Often loud, emphatic. May continue even with no listener. Classic mania finding.
🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Speech
ManiaPressured — or rapid with increased quantity
AnxietyIncreased quantity, rapid rate
MDDDecreased quantity, soft, monotone, reduced prosody
SchizophreniaDecreased quantity, monotone, non-spontaneous, increased latency
Substance IntoxSlurred (dysarthric), garbled speech

Thought Process
HOW thoughts connect — the form and flow of thinking, not the content
📋

Document proportionally: "Mostly logical and sequential, with ~25% tangentiality" is far more useful than a binary label.

→

✅ Goal-Directed / Logical

Answers the question; clear word and sentence structure; logical linkage between ideas; gets to the point.

🌀 Circumstantial

Over-inclusive of irrelevant details — eventually reaches the point but takes a long, winding path.

↗️ Tangential

Never reaches the original point; goes off on topics triggered by stimuli; words are understandable.

🚀 Flight of Ideas

Rapid near-continuous speech with abrupt topic changes based on understandable word associations; listener can usually follow; seen with pressured speech in mania.

🌪️ Loose Associations & Beyond

  • Connection between ideas is unclear/nonsensical — listener cannot follow
  • Fragmentation: phrases intact but disconnected — meaningless sentences
  • Word salad: words intact, all sentence structure lost
  • Incoherence: unintelligible words — no phrases or structure
Rate & Flow Abnormalities
  • Racing thoughts: too fast to articulate — "like watching 3 TVs at once"
  • Perseveration: repeating same responses despite new questions
  • Thought blocking: abrupt interruption before idea completes, then resumes
  • Derailment: stops suddenly, restarts on unrelated topic
🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Thought Process
SchizophreniaDerailment, loose associations, fragmentation, word salad if severe
Mania / HypomaniaRacing thoughts + flight of ideas; often with pressured speech
DeliriumAny disorder of thought process; fluctuating
DementiaErrors in word choice and grammar

Thought Content
WHAT the patient is thinking — 6 critical domains to assess
🚨 A. Suicidal Ideation
Thoughts of DeathContemplates implications; thinks about funeral; passive musing
Passive SI"Wishes to sleep and not wake up" — no intent to act
Active SIWants to die now; with or without specific plan (plan → HPI)
⚠️ B. Homicidal Ideation
ThoughtsThinks about killing/hurting another; considers implications
Active HIWants to kill a specific person today; plan details → HPI
C. Delusions
🧠

Definition: A false belief held despite incontrovertible contradicting evidence, not explained by culture or religion. Bizarre delusions = physically impossible by cultural standards.

Persecutory / Paranoid

Being attacked, harassed, persecuted, conspired against. Most common.

Referential

Random events hold special personal significance ("Cubs losing is a signal to evacuate").

Grandiose

Inflated worth, power, or special relationship to deity or celebrity.

Erotomanic

A higher-status person is in love with the patient.

Somatic

False beliefs involving bodily functions or sensations.

Bizarre Types

Thought insertion / withdrawal / broadcasting · Delusion of control

D. Hallucinations

Types

  • Auditory: must be in clear sensorium; command AH — can they resist?
  • Visual: false visual perceptions — common in delirium, SUD
  • Tactile: Formication — bugs crawling under skin (cocaine)
  • Olfactory/Gustatory: false smell or taste perceptions

Key Distinctions

  • Hypnagogic: while falling asleep — NOT abnormal (mnemonic: "go" to sleep)
  • Hypnopompic: while waking — NOT abnormal (mnemonic: "m" for morning)
  • Illusions: misinterpretation of a real stimulus — not a hallucination
  • Hallucinosis: patient knows perceptions are not real — insight intact
E & F. Obsessions/Compulsions · Derealization/Depersonalization

Obsessions & Compulsions

Obsessions: intrusive, ego-dystonic thoughts causing distress (contamination, harm, doubt).

Compulsions: repetitive behaviors or mental acts (washing, checking, counting) to reduce obsession-driven anxiety.

Derealization & Depersonalization

Derealization: surroundings feel unreal, dreamlike, foggy, visually distorted.

Depersonalization: detachment from one's own body/mind — "watching myself from outside." Both typically co-occur. Common in PTSD.

🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Thought Content
SchizophreniaBizarre delusions; AH (often command); thought insertion/broadcasting
OCDEgo-dystonic obsessions + compulsions; insight often preserved
PTSDFlashbacks, derealization, depersonalization
Cocaine IntoxFormication — tactile hallucinations ("coke bugs")
ETOH WithdrawalHallucinosis — insight intact (knows they're not real)
Delusional DisorderNon-bizarre delusions; no other psychotic symptoms

Cognition
Orientation · Level of Consciousness · Attention · Memory · Abstract Thinking
Oriented × 4
Person

Knows who they are and who is around them? Absent only in most severe cases.

Place

Knows WHERE they are AND behaves accordingly?

Time

Approximate date and time of day; behavior consistent with present moment?

Situation

Oriented to purpose of interview; overlaps significantly with Insight.

Level of Consciousness
Alert

Fully awake, appropriately responsive

Clouding

Impaired awareness of environment

Somnolent

Drowsy; delayed or incomplete responses

Stupor

Vigorous, repeated stimulation required to rouse

Coma

No verbal or motor response to noxious stimuli

Cognitive Domains

Concentration & Attention

Serial 7s (100 – 7 – 7…) · Spell WORLD / EARTH forwards then backwards

Memory

  • Recent: recall news events from past months
  • Remote: childhood data, personal history (verify!)
  • Recall: 3 unrelated words after 3–5 min — apple · honesty · star

Abstract Thinking

Proverb interpretation ("rolling stone gathers no moss") · Similarities/differences ("how are a bush and a tree alike?") · Concrete = takes everything literally

Formal Screens

  • MMSE (Folstein) — 30 points · bedside validated
  • SLUMS — sensitive for mild cognitive impairment
  • MoCA — most sensitive for MCI; 30 points
🔬 Diagnostic Pearls — Cognition
DeliriumDisoriented to time/place/situation; very poor concentration; serial MMSE
DementiaMemory impairment, poor abstraction, apraxia, agnosia
MDDPoor concentration, indecisive — can mimic dementia ("pseudodementia")
ManiaDistractible, poor concentration despite high energy
KorsakoffAnterograde amnesia; poor short-term memory; confabulation
Substance IntoxDecreased alertness, disoriented, poor concentration
9
Judgment
Current/recent ability to assess situations correctly and act appropriately within reality
⚖️

Judgment examines recent decisions and choices, not hypothetical problem-solving. Asking about real behavior ("What did you do when…?") is more clinically relevant than the theater-fire hypothetical.

What to Assess
  • Recent choices that led to current presentation
  • Ability to make plans consistent with reality
  • Response to hypothetical scenarios (less preferred)
  • Awareness of consequences of behavior
📌 Poor Judgment Examples
  • Acquiring weapon to self-harm
  • Acting on delusions (calling exterminator for formication)
  • Spending thousands impulsively during manic episode
  • Stopping medications abruptly without discussion
Rating Scale
Good / IntactDecisions appropriate; acts within safety and social norms
Fair / PartialSome appropriate decisions; inconsistent; limited follow-through
Poor / ImpairedDecisions clearly inconsistent with reality or safety
✍️ Documentation Examples
  • "Judgment: Poor — actively delusional; planning to harm identified individual"
  • "Judgment: Fair/Poor — currently denies delusions but was acting on them yesterday"
  • "Judgment: Fair — acknowledges need for treatment but has not followed through"
⚠️

Critical: A patient who attempted suicide 3 hours ago and is now denying SI — judgment cannot be described as "good" in isolation. Always contextualize with recent behavior.

10
Insight
To what degree does the patient understand they are ill — and can act on that understanding?
Insight Spectrum
POOR 🚫
Complete Denial
Refuses to acknowledge illness; blames others, external factors, or organic causes for symptoms.
PARTIAL 🌗
Partial Awareness
Acknowledges something is wrong but simultaneously denies it. May have Intellectual Insight — understands cognitively they are ill but cannot apply this to make behavioral changes.
GOOD ✅
True / Full Insight
Understands the objective reality of their situation AND has the motivation and emotional impetus to make useful behavioral changes. Actively working with treatment team.
✍️ Documentation Examples
  • "Insight: Poor — stopped medications, now actively manic, refusing treatment"
  • "Insight: Fair — acknowledges depression and SI but declines treatment"
  • "Insight: Fair — agrees to accept help after involuntary admission last night"
  • "Insight: Good — clear understanding of illness, actively engaged in treatment planning"
🔑 Key Principle

In psychiatry, the default is that Judgment and Insight are intact. You are assessing for evidence of impairment.

A single area of impaired judgment (e.g., actively suicidal) can have dire consequences — document thoroughly even when overall functioning appears intact. Always consider both current and recent events together.
