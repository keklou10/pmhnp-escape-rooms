📋 Patient Scenario — Initial Presentation

M.L., a 34-year-old African American female, is referred to your outpatient PMHNP clinic by her primary care provider with a chief complaint of "I just can't get out of bed anymore — I've felt this way for almost two months."

She reports 7 weeks of persistent depressed mood occurring most of the day, nearly every day. Associated symptoms include hypersomnia (sleeping 12–14 hours/day, yet never feeling rested), hyperphagia with a 17 lb weight gain, marked psychomotor retardation, profound anhedonia (she has withdrawn entirely from her book club and stopped painting, which she previously described as her "lifeline"), significant cognitive slowing ("my brain feels like it's running through mud"), and passive death wishes ("I don't want to kill myself, but I wouldn't mind if I just didn't wake up"). She denies active suicidal ideation, plan, or intent.

Past Psychiatric History: One prior psychiatric hospitalization at age 27 — chart notes describe a 5-day period of dramatically decreased sleep (2–3 hrs/night) without fatigue, grandiosity ("I felt like I had discovered the secret to everything"), pressured speech, hypersexuality, and impulsive spending (maxed out two credit cards in 48 hours). She was hospitalized voluntarily after her sister intervened. Discharge diagnosis was recorded as "Rule Out Mood Disorder NOS." She was never prescribed medication and never followed up with outpatient psychiatry.

Current Medications: None. Allergies: NKDA. Substance Use: Occasional wine (1–2 glasses/week); denies illicit drug use. Family History: Mother treated for "depression" (diagnosis unclear); maternal uncle with a confirmed mood disorder, deceased by suicide at age 52. Social History: Works as a middle school art teacher; currently on FMLA. Lives alone in a one-bedroom apartment. Close relationship with her younger sister who lives 10 minutes away.

Medical History: Hypothyroidism (TSH checked 3 months ago — normal on no medication per patient report; chart confirms euthyroid). No cardiovascular, hepatic, or renal disease. BMI: 29.4 (elevated from baseline of 26.5).
